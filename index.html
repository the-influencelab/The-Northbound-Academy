What Northbound sets out to do

Backing the next generation before we lose them

Right now, across Yorkshire, there are young adults who are bright, funny, creative and capable, sitting at home wondering why no one has called them back. Some have sent dozens of applications and heard nothing. Some have qualifications and still feel like they don't belong in a room full of professionals. Some have quietly stopped trying.

They haven't run out of potential. They've run out of people who believe in them out loud.

Northbound exists to change that. It gives young adults the inner layer that school, college and university rarely have time for: knowing who they are, steadying themselves under pressure, speaking with confidence, and walking into a workplace feeling they have every right to be there.

Then it opens the doors. Real employers, real conversations, paid reverse mentoring and placements that turn confidence into a career.

Why this is personal

I left Doncaster to find my future. I've come home so the next generation doesn't have to.

"Northbound gives young people a reason to stay, not the reason I had to leave."

I grew up in Doncaster and, like so many young people here, I left for the bright lights. That journey took me to Manchester and around the world. I've lived and worked in eight countries, from Colombia and Thailand to California and Vietnam, and spent over thirty years in leadership and people development.

Along the way I've built sales methodologies, led one of the UK's largest change programmes, and worked with major brands, leaders and young adults on resilience, confidence and the imposter phenomenon.

The work that moved me most was with young adults. I've watched someone who could barely make eye contact stand up and tell their story to a room of business leaders. I've seen young people teach a director something they never knew. I've seen workshops and honest conversations change the direction of someone's working life.

That's what I'm bringing home. Everything I've learnt, rebuilt for Doncaster and South Yorkshire, for the young adults who deserve the same chances I had to travel so far to find.

What it feels like to be here

A place to connect, laugh, be heard and grow

Northbound isn't a classroom and it isn't a lecture. It's a room where young people feel safe enough to be themselves, and brave enough to try something new.

Fun

Energy, movement, games, stories and real laughter. People learn best when they're enjoying themselves, so every day is designed to feel alive.

Connection

Young people meet each other, local leaders, guest speakers and mentors. Friendships form, networks grow, and no one walks this road alone.

Empathy

Every young person is met where they are, without judgement. Their story matters, their feelings make sense, and their potential is taken seriously.

Some sessions take place outdoors in restorative settings such as Potteric Carr, Cusworth Park and Sandall Beat Wood, because some of the best conversations happen away from a desk.

Monday in Doncaster

Twelve young adults walk into a room. One has sent thirty applications and heard back twice. One hasn't phoned a stranger in months. One is a graduate stacking shelves.

By Friday, each of them has told their story to local business leaders, taught a director something new, and left with a plan, a mentor and a way to steady themselves when it matters.

What Northbound is

Three hubs, one academy

1

Confidence and community

For young adults who need belonging, confidence and a safe place to start. Funded, so young people never pay.

2

Paid reverse mentoring

Skilled young adults are paid to mentor leaders on AI tools, social platforms, Gen Z insight and digital ways of working.

3

Collective work

Shadowing and placements with Yorkshire employers, plus a bolt on Sales Academy into local contact centres.

Running through all three: personal identity, self efficacy and action orientated goals.

A collective effort

Northbound is not about one town or one organisation. It's about all of us working together to open doors that have stayed closed for too long. It will connect with sixth forms and colleges across Doncaster, and with universities across Yorkshire and the North, adding the inner layer that helps young people's skills and qualifications land.

This is about social mobility. Where a young person grows up should never decide how far they can go.

A city on the move

A city for abundant personal development

Doncaster is changing faster than at any point in a generation. A newly named city, a growing tech community, Gateway One opening its doors as a digital hub, and the airport set to return, bringing jobs and connections far beyond Yorkshire.

This is an up and coming city, and the North is rising with it. The investment is building the workplaces. Northbound builds the people ready to walk into them.
